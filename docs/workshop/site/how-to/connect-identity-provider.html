Add your institution's sign-in to Keycloak (general Keycloak guidance)

General Keycloak steps for letting people sign in to the CSOC, and to every commons that uses it, with their institutional accounts.

When you need this

When people other than the local admin user need to sign in, through campus single sign-on or a federation such as InCommon. This page gives the general Keycloak procedure for identity brokering; the exact screens depend on your Keycloak version, and the authority is "Identity Brokering" in the Keycloak Server Administration Guide.

Before you start

  • Ask your identity team whether they offer SAML 2.0 or OpenID Connect, and what they need from you to register a service. For SAML they need Keycloak's service provider metadata; for OpenID Connect they issue a client ID and secret.
  • Agree which attributes they release. Gen3 identifies users by email, so the email attribute must be released and must match the emails in each commons' user.yaml.
  • Your address must be in the allowed addresses, because the Keycloak administration console is reached through auth.csoc.example.org.
  • AWS console Get Keycloak's administration login from AWS Secrets Manager, under gen3-csoc/csoc-dev/.

Steps

  1. Browser Sign in to the Keycloak administration console at https://auth.csoc.example.org/admin/ with that login.
  2. Switch to the realm csoc-realm.
  3. Open Identity providers and add a provider of the type your identity team offers: SAML v2.0 or OpenID Connect v1.0.
  4. Give your identity team the redirect URI or the SAML metadata that Keycloak shows for the new provider, and enter the client ID and secret, or the metadata URL, that they give you.
  5. Add mappers so that the email, and the first and last names, are copied from the provider's attributes into the Keycloak user.
  6. Sign out, open portal.csoc.example.org, and choose the new provider on the sign-in page.

Check it worked

You can sign in to the CSOC portal through your institution's sign-in, and Keycloak lists your account under Users in csoc-realm, linked to the provider. Commons that use Use CSOC login accept the same sign-in; what each user may do there still comes from its user.yaml.

Protect the administrator accounts

Require a second factor for the Keycloak administration login and the CSOC's admin user. Keycloak supports one-time passwords as a required action and in authentication flows; see the Server Administration Guide.

If it fails

  • The admin console does not load: add your address; see Allow a new IP address.
  • Sign-in works but commons deny access: compare the email Keycloak shows for the user with the email in the commons' user.yaml.
