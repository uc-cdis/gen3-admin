Deploy a Gen3 commons

Use the portal's Gen3 deployment wizard to install a first commons, with sign-in through the CSOC.

Where CSOC portalTime about 12 minutes from Deploy Gen3

Goal

Install a Gen3 commons named commons-a into the CSOC's cluster, at commons-a.csoc.example.org, with sign-in through the CSOC's Keycloak and administrator rights for jdoe@example.org.

A commons is made of Gen3 services, among them Fence (sign-in and access tokens), Arborist (permissions), Indexd (the index of data files) and Sheepdog (metadata submission).

Steps

Prepare the user.yaml

  1. Copy the user.yaml below.
  2. Replace both occurrences of <admin-email> with the email you sign in with, for example jdoe@example.org: once under users: in the administrators group, and once under users: at the end.
  3. Keep it at hand; you paste the whole file into the Auth step.
yaml
authz:
  anonymous_policies: []
  all_users_policies: []
  groups:
    - name: administrators
      policies:
        - all_programs_admin
        - services.sheepdog-admin
        - indexd_admin
        - data_upload
      users:
        - <admin-email>
  resources:
    - name: data_file
    - name: programs
    - name: services
      subresources:
        - name: sheepdog
          subresources:
            - name: submission
              subresources:
                - name: program
                - name: project
        - name: indexd
          subresources:
            - name: admin
  policies:
    - id: all_programs_admin
      resource_paths: [/programs]
      role_ids: [admin]
    - id: services.sheepdog-admin
      resource_paths:
        - /services/sheepdog/submission/program
        - /services/sheepdog/submission/project
      role_ids: [sheepdog_admin]
    - id: indexd_admin
      resource_paths: [/services/indexd/admin]
      role_ids: [indexd_admin]
    - id: data_upload
      resource_paths: [/data_file]
      role_ids: [file_uploader]
  roles:
    - id: admin
      permissions:
        - id: admin
          action: {service: "*", method: "*"}
    - id: sheepdog_admin
      permissions:
        - id: sheepdog_admin_action
          action: {service: sheepdog, method: "*"}
    - id: indexd_admin
      permissions:
        - id: indexd_admin
          action: {service: indexd, method: "*"}
    - id: file_uploader
      permissions:
        - id: file_upload
          action: {service: fence, method: file_upload}

users:
  <admin-email>:
    admin: true

In plain terms, this file does four things:

  • It gives nobody access by default. Anonymous visitors, and signed-in users without a policy, can do nothing.
  • It gives your email full control of all programs and projects, including creating them and submitting metadata.
  • It lets your email manage the index of data files and upload data files.
  • It marks your email as a commons administrator.

To give a colleague the same rights, add their email as a second line under users: in the administrators group, and add them under users: at the end with admin: true. The user.yaml guide describes other permissions.

Press Deploy Gen3 straight away

The portal's access token is short-lived. If the page stays open too long, pressing Deploy Gen3 shows Deployment Failed with the message "Token verification failed". Nothing is installed in that case: reload the page or sign in again, check the summary, and press Deploy Gen3 again.

Open the commons

  1. When the portal shows Gen3 Deployed Successfully!, open the address under "Access your instance at:", https://commons-a.csoc.example.org.
  2. If the browser shows 502 Bad Gateway, wait 5 minutes and reload: the services are still starting (see the note under Expected result).
  3. Press Login, and sign in through the CSOC's Keycloak as admin.

Done when: commons-a.csoc.example.org shows the Gen3 portal, signed in as admin, whose email is jdoe@example.org.

Where the data lives

A Gen3 commons keeps metadata in its database and data files in object storage. This tutorial's commons has a database, on an EBS volume with persistence on, but no data bucket: Data Upload Bucket on the Globals step is empty, and Crossplane, which could create one, is off. Uploading data files needs an S3 bucket and Fence access to it, which the tutorial does not create; see Known gaps.

A commons also adds cost on top of the CSOC: the EC2 nodes Karpenter starts for its services, its own Application Load Balancer, and its EBS volume. See What a commons adds.

Deploy a second commons

On the success page, press Deploy Another and repeat the steps with the release name commons-b and the hostname commons-b.csoc.example.org.

Expected result

The portal shows Deploying Gen3, with "Sending configuration to cluster" and a grid of deployments with ready and total counts. When the release is installed, it shows Gen3 Deployed Successfully! with the status, chart and revision, and the buttons Go to Dashboard and Deploy Another.

A 502 error for several minutes is expected

Right after the deploy, commons-a.csoc.example.org returns 502 Bad Gateway for several minutes. The load balancer is ready before the services behind it: arborist and indexd wait for their database secrets before they start. The site answers once they are ready.

If the deploy takes longer than the portal waits, it shows Deployment Timeout. That is not a failure; follow the deployments in the dashboard.

Time

About 12 minutes from Deploy Gen3 to a working site, including several minutes of 502 responses while the services start; normal up to 20 minutes.

What's happening

Before the install, the CSOC requests an ACM certificate for the commons and registers the commons with Keycloak, so that the commons can sign users in through the CSOC. When you press Deploy Gen3, the CSOC sends the settings to the agent, and the agent installs the Gen3 Helm chart into the commons' own namespace. The commons' services start, Karpenter adds nodes if they do not fit, a load balancer is created with the certificate, and external-dns publishes the hostname in your zone.

For the details, see Deploying a commons. To change permissions later, see Edit a commons' user.yaml.
